About

My research asks how data and analytical methods can improve decisions in healthcare, for patients, clinicians, healthcare managers and policymakers alike. I develop methods in decision analytics, optimisation, machine learning and health economics, and apply them with clinical partners to chronic disease, cancer, organ transplantation and the organisation of care.

I am a member of the Centre for Health Economics & Policy Innovation (CHEPI). Before joining Imperial in 2018, I was a Senior Research Analyst in the Department of Medicine at the University of Chicago. I received my PhD from the Sauder School of Business at the University of British Columbia.

Research themes

Personalised and stratified care

Treatment plans that learn from each patient's data, and care designs that are tailored yet simple enough to implement.

Designing care for chronic disease

When and how to monitor, screen and intervene in conditions such as kidney disease, cancer and cystic fibrosis.

The value of medical innovations

Cost-effectiveness of new medicines, devices, diagnostics and models of care, especially in diabetes.

The organisation of care

Clinician workload, new care models and decision support, from primary care to heart transplantation.
