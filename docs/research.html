Research

Better decisions for healthcare

My research brings decision analytics, optimisation, machine learning and health economics to four connected problems. The work is collaborative, with clinicians, health economists and data scientists at Imperial, in the NHS and at universities in the United States.

Personalised and stratified care

Clinical guidelines often treat patients alike, yet patients differ in how their disease progresses and how they respond to care. Bayes-adaptive treatment design learns about each patient's disease trajectory as data accumulate and adapts treatment accordingly. Stratified care groups patients so that care is tailored to clinically meaningful groups while remaining simple enough to implement. Applications include preparing patients with kidney disease for dialysis and risk-adapted follow-up after cancer treatment, where tailored schedules can detect recurrence earlier without additional scans.

Designing care for chronic disease

This work asks when and how to monitor, screen and intervene. Examples include the timing of vascular access for patients approaching dialysis, surveillance for cancer recurrence combining imaging with blood tests for tumour DNA, and screening for hearing loss in people with cystic fibrosis.

The value of medical innovations

Cost-effectiveness and decision models estimate how new technologies affect lifetime health outcomes and costs. Studies cover continuous glucose monitoring and insulin pumps, genetic testing for monogenic diabetes, shared telemedicine appointments for young adults with type 1 diabetes, first-line diabetes medicines, and a new multi-ethnic model of type 2 diabetes outcomes in the US. Several of these studies have been taken up in guidelines, payer resources and practice; see Impact.

The organisation of care

This strand studies pressures on clinicians and how care can be delivered sustainably: the time needed to provide recommended primary care, the productivity required for medical scribes to be cost-neutral, and how emergency operating-theatre performance is measured. In heart transplantation, machine-learning models built on US national registry data aim to support decisions about accepting a donor heart.

Current directions

  • Decision support for heart transplantation. Moving from prediction to learning organ-acceptance strategies from historical offer data with causal reinforcement learning, with colleagues at Carnegie Mellon University and the Medical University of South Carolina.
  • Hierarchical optimisation for stratified care. Machine-learning and reinforcement-learning methods for bilevel problems, where the best way to group patients depends on the best care for each group.
  • Information design for diagnostics. How diagnostic tests themselves should be designed, balancing missed cases against false alarms, from the perspectives of patients, manufacturers and policymakers.
